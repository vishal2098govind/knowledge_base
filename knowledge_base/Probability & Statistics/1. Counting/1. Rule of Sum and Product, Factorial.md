### Rule of Sum
#rule-of-sum
- If a sequence of tasks $T_{1}, T_{2}, T_{3} ... T_{m}$ can be done $W_{1}, W_{2}, W_{3} ... W_{m}$ ways respectively
- and **none of the tasks can be performed simultaneously**
- Then, the no.of ways to perform **one of these** tasks: $W_{1}+W_{2}+W_{3}+\dots+W_{m}$
- "**one of the**" inherently means **OR** i.e. either $T_{1}$ or $T_{2}$ or $T_{3}$ or $....$$T_{m}$
- $OR => +$
- E.g.: There 4 types of cookies and 3 types of cakes, no.of ways to select one of them?
	- $T_{1}$: eat a cookie. $W_{1}$ = 4 as there are 4 cookies so 4 ways to select a cookie
	- $T_{2}$: eat a cake. $W_{2}$ = 3 as there are 3 cake so 3 ways to select a cake
	- no.of ways of selecting **one of them** 
		- i.e. no.of ways of selecting either a cookie **OR** a cake 
		- = $W_{1}+W_{2} = 4 + 3 = 7$
		- i.e. let's say 
			- 4 cookies = C1 C2 C3 C4
			- 3 cakes = A1 A2 A3
			- ways of selecting either a cookie **OR** a cake
				- C1 or C2 or C3 or C4
				- or A1 or A2 or A3

### Rule of Product
#rule-of-product
- If a sequence of tasks $T_{1}, T_{2}, T_{3} ... T_{m}$ can be done $W_{1}, W_{2}, W_{3} ... W_{m}$ ways respectively
- and **every task is performed sequentially** i.e. $T_{1}$ then $T_{2}$ then $T_{3}$ ... then $T_{m}$
- Then, the no.of ways to perform all tasks : $W_{1}*W_{2}*W_{3}*\dots*Wm$
- **sequentially** inherently means **AND** i.e. $T_{1}$ and then $T_{2}$ and then $T_{3}$ ... and then $T_{m}$
- $AND => *$
- E.g.: There 4 types of cookies and 3 types of cakes, no.of ways to select one of them?
	- $T_{1}$: eat a cookie. $W_{1}$ = 4 as there are 4 cookies so 4 ways to select a cookie
	- $T_{2}$: eat a cake. $W_{2}$ = 3 as there are 3 cake so 3 ways to select a cake
	- no.of ways of selecting **all of them**
		- i.e. no.of ways of selecting a cooky **AND** cake
		- = $W_{1}*W_{2} = 4 * 3 = 12$
		- i.e. let's say 
			- 4 cookies = C1 C2 C3 C4
			- 3 cakes = A1 A2 A3
			- ways of selecting a cooky **AND** cake : 
				- C1A1 C1A2 C1A3
				- C2A1 C2A2 C2A3
				- C3A1 C3A2 C3A3
				- C4A1 C4A2 C4A3


### Questions
- Question: There are three places
	- X: Home, Y: chai-point, Z: school
	- X - Y : 3 buses, 2 autos
	- Y - Z : 4 buses, 5 autos
	- no.of ways to reach Z from X?
- Solution:
	- To go from X to Z : 
		- $T_{X - Y}$ **AND** then $T_{Y - Z}$
		- $W_{x-y}*W_{y-z}$ (rule of product)
		- To go from X-Y: either bus **OR** auto: 3+2 (rule of sum)
		- To go from Y-Z: either bus **OR** auto: 4+5 (rule of sum)
		- To go from X-Z = $(3+2)*(4+5)=45$

- Question: How many divisors of 100 are there? #no-of-divisors
- Solution:
	- 2, 5, 10, 100, 1, 4, 25, 20, 50
	- Trick: do using LCM or **prime factorisation**
		- 100: $2^2 * 5^2$
		- Now, to find all divisors of 100 or $2^2 * 5^2$
			- we need to add some numbers in denominators under $2^2 * 5^2$
			- we need to only add those numbers which will not leave any denominator after dividing
			- i.e. 
				- $2^0 * 5^0 = 1$
				- or $2^1 * 5^0 = 2$ 
				- or $2^2 * 5^0=4$ 
				- or $2^0 * 5^1=5$ 
				- or  $2^1 * 5^1=10$
				- or  $2^2 * 5^1=20$
				- or  $2^0 * 5^2=100$
				- or  $2^1 * 5^2=1$
				- or  $2^2 * 5^2=1$
			- $= 2^{0,1,2} * 5^{0,1,2}$
			- How is Rule of Sum or Rule of Product applied?
				- $T_{1}$: use 2, $W_{1}$: no.of ways we can use 2 = 3 (0, 1, 2)
				- $T_{1}$: use 5, $W_{1}$: no.of ways we can use 5 = 3 (0, 1, 2)
				- No.of ways we can use 2 **AND then** 5 = $W_{1}*W_{2}$ = 3 * 3 = 9
		- Example for 360
			- 360 = $2^3*3^2*5$ => no.of divisors of 360 = $4*3*2$ = 24
	- Trick: if  $n = p_{1}^{e_{1}}*p_{2}^{e_{2}}*p_{3}^{e_{3}} \dots p_{k}^{e_{k}}$ , where $p_{i}$ are prime factors of n, 
		- then no.of divisors 
			- = $(e_{1}+1)+(e_{2}+1)+(e_{3}+1)+(e_{4}+1)+\dots+(e_{k}+1)$
			- = $\sum_{i=0}^{k}  (e^{i}+1)$

- Question: Sum of all divisors of 100?
- Solution:
	- 100 = $5^2*2^2$
	- Divisors: 
		- $2^05^0$, $2^05^1$
		- $2^15^0$, $2^15^1$
		- Sum: $2^05^0$ + $2^05^1$ + $2^15^0$ + $2^15^1$ = ($2^0$ + $2^1$)($5^0+5^1$) = Product of two GP (Geometric Progression)
		- GP Formula: $GP = a(\frac{r^n-1}{r-1})$, a is first term
			- ($2^0$ + $2^1$) = $2^0(\frac{2^2-1}{2-1})$ = 3
			- ($5^0$ + $5^1$) = $5^0(\frac{5^2-1}{5-1})$ = 6
		- Sum of divisors = 3x6 = 18
	- Sum of divisors = $\prod_{i=1}^k\left( \frac{p_{i}^{e_{i+1}}-1}{p_{i}-1} \right)$ for $n = p_{1}^{e_{1}} * p_{2}^{e_{2}} * p_{3}^{e_{3}} \dots p_{k}^{e_{k}}$

### Factorial
$n!$ = $n*(n-1)*(n-2)*(n-3)\dots 3*2*1$ = $\prod_{i=0}^{n-1} (n-i)$
Why to learn $n!$?
- No.of ways of arranging n different objects in a row
- `_1_,_2_,_3_,_4_,_5_,_6_,...._n_`
- $T1$: Put an item on first place, 
	- $W_{1}$: no.of ways of putting an item in first place = $n$
- **AND then** $T2$: Put n-1 items on remaining n-1 places ( #rule-of-product ) - this is a recursive problem

### Questions:
- Question: How many factors does 9! has?
- Solutions:
	- $9! = 3^2,2^3, 7, 2*3, 5, 2^2, 3*1, 2 = 3^4*2^7*7*5 = (4+1)*(7+1)*(1+1)*(1+1) = 5*8*2*2=160$

- Question: What is the number of 0s at the end of 100! ?
- Solutions:
	- 0s at the end can only be created using one 2 and one 5 which is one 10
	- no.of 0s at the end = min of no.of (2, 5)
	- for $2^3*5^2*7*13^3$, no.of 0s at the end = 2 i.e. min(3, 2)
	- thus, just find the power of 2 and 5 in the prime factorisation of 100!
		- mostly there will be more number of 2s than number of 5s
		- thus, mostly calculating **number of 5s** should be enough to find no.of 0s
			- to calculate number of 5s, only the multiples of 5s will have 5 as factors
			- thus, in 50!, only 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50 will have 5s
			- i.e. $12$ 5s are in 50!, i.e. 50! has 12 0s
