- **Question**: Your mother-in-law buys 1000 small gifts (each are diff) to relatives for Christmas. There are 25 relatives to gift to. How many ways to distribute such that all gifts are distributed and no gift is left?
- **Solution**:
	- 1000 **different** gifts distribute among 25 relatives
	- each gift can be distributed in 25 ways, thus no.of ways to distribute = $25^{1000}$
		- T1: distribute G1 among 25 = no.of ways of doing that = 25 
			- (either give G1 to $R1$ **OR** to $R2$ **OR** $R3$ **OR** .... **OR** to $R_{25}$) #rule-of-sum 
		- **and then** T2: distribute G2 among 25 #rule-of-product 
			- (either give G2 to $R1$ **OR** to $R2$ **OR** $R3$ **OR** .... **OR** to $R_{25}$)
		- ... **and then** T100: distribute G1000 among 25 relatives = 25 #rule-of-product 
			- (either give G1000 to $R1$ **OR** to $R2$ **OR** $R3$ **OR** .... **OR** to $R_{25}$) #rule-of-sum 
		- i.e. $25^{1000}$
	- here #combination-with-repetition cannot be applied because, each gift is different and not identical
		- $n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}+\dots+n_{25}=1000$ can be applied only if each of 1000 objects are identical
	- here, $1000*999*998\dots *976$ also cannot be applied because, this way we are only giving 25 gifts and each relative only gets one gift
		- this is nothing but $^{1000}P_{25}$
		- $^nP_{r}$ is nothing but no.of ways of distributing $r$ objects among $n$ containers/people #pnc 
	- Understand with smaller example:
		 - 5 **DIFFERENT** gifts among 3 relatives, such that
			- **CASE 1: no.of ways to distribute 1 unique gift to each of 3 relative without repetition**
				- $^5P_{3}$ i.e for R1 we have 5 options, for R2 we have 4 options and for R3 we have 3 options
				- here we need not distribute every gift, we can end up with some un-distributed gifts
			- **CASE 2: no.of ways to distribute all 5 DIFFERENT gifts among 3 relative**
				- if here nothing else is mentioned => repetition not allowed
				- here it's not $^5P_{3}$ because each relative need not get exactly 1 gift, rather can get any number of gifts
				- here all gifts are to be distributed, we cannot end up with any un-distributed gifts
				- thus, **for every gift we have 3 choices among relatives**
				- thus, no.of such ways = $3^5$
			- **CASE 3: no.of ways of distributing all 5 gifts such that each relative gets one gift and repetition is allowed**
				- since repetition is allowed, it means that there is **infinite supply of each of 5 types of gifts**
				- thus **for every relative we have 5 choices among gift types**
				- thus no.of such ways = $5^3$
			- **CASE 4: no.of ways of distributing such that R1 gets 2 gifts, R2 gets 2 gifts and R3 gets 5 gifts**
				- $^5C_{2}*^3C_{2}*^1C_{1}$ => #multinomial-coefficient  is a **coefficient $R_{1}^2*R_{2}^2*R_{3}$ of multinomial expression of $(R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3})^5$**
					- **T1**: no.of ways of choosing 2 gifts from available 5 gifts for R1
					- **and then T2**: no.of ways of choosing 2 gifts from **remaining** 3 gifts for R2
					- **and then T3**: no.of ways of choosing 1 gift from the **remaining** 1 gift for R3 #rule-of-product 
			- Since gifts here are **different** and not **identical**, 
				- we cannot apply the equation of n1+n2+n3=5 and n1,n2,n3 >= 0 #combination-with-repetition 
				- this equation is applied when it doesn't matter which particular gift is being given to which relative, this is possible only if all gifts are identical, 
				- if all are identical, only count matters i.e. how many gifts are given to whom, not which particular type of gift is given to whom
				- if we have **5 identical gifts** and distribute among **3 relatives** with **repetition allowed** #combination-with-repetition 
					- n1+n2+n3 = 5
					- 3 partitions, thus 2 sticks and thus 2 boxes for sticks
					- remaining boxes should add up for holding 5 gifts, thus need 5 boxes, thus 5+2=7 boxes in total
					- thus, ans = $^7C_{2}$
			- **CASE 6: if we have 5 identical gifts and distribute among 3 relatives such that R1 gets 2 gifts R2 gets 2 gifts and R3 gets 1 gift**
				- R1+R2+R3
				- ans = 1 way only since all are identical
			- **CASE 7: if we have 5 identical gifts and distribute among 3 relatives such that each relative gets at least 1 gift**
				- (R1 + 1) +(R2 + 1)+(R3 + 1) = 5
				- R1 + R2 + R3 = 2
				- thus, 3 partitions, 2 sticks, 2 boxes, and remaining boxes for adding up to 2, thus 2+2 = 4 boxes
				- $^4C_{2}$
			- **CASE: 5 different gifts distribute among 3 relatives such that each relative gets at least 1 gift**
				- uses #inclusion/exclusion
